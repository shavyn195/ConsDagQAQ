#### Abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) is central for aligning large lan-
guage models (LLMs) with complex reasoning, yet the lead-
ing critic-free algorithm, Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO), broadcasts a single sequence-level advantage uni-
formly across long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generations and
constrains exploration with an unbounded Kullback-Leibler
(KL) penalty, which dilutes token-level supervision and in-
duces gradient instability and mode-seeking conservatism.
We propose TIDE (Token-level Importance via Deviation
and Entropy), a critic-free RL framework that reinterprets
distribution deviation as a guiding signal rather than a rigid
penalty. TIDE replaces the volatile reverse KL divergence
with the bounded Hellinger distance, safely quantifying
token-level exploration without risk of gradient explosion.
To separate genuine reasoning breakthroughs from confi-
dent hallucinations, an entropy gating mechanism scales
this deviation by the policy’s predictive uncertainty, and
the gated scores redistribute the sequence-level advantage
across tokens. The resulting updates amplify exploratory
steps while suppressing low-entropy deviations, without a
value network and with negligible overhead. Across in-
domain, held-out, and out-of-domain benchmarks, TIDE out-
performs strong critic-free baselines: on the Qwen2.5-32B
backbone it reaches 60.0% and 46.0% Avg@32 accuracy on
AIME 2024 and AIME 2025, respectively, above competitive
baselines such as DAPO. Token-level attribution, causal per-
turbation, and exploration analyses further support the inter-
pretation that the entropy-gated deviation identifies pivotal
reasoning steps rather than merely perturbing optimization
dynamics.

#### 1. Introduction

Aligning large language models (LLMs) with reinforce-
ment learning (RL) has fundamentally advanced their ca-
pacity for complex problem solving and logical reasoning
[9, 24]. Among recent paradigms, Group Relative Policy
Optimization (GRPO) [31] is particularly successful at elicit-
ing long-horizon Chain-of-Thought (CoT) behavior without
an auxiliary value network: by optimizing the policy rel-
ative to a group of sampled responses, it reduces memory
overhead while remaining competitive on mathematical and
algorithmic reasoning [9]. In this setting, models learn to
self-correct and explore extended logical trajectories purely
through scalar rewards from verifiers or reward models.
Despite these advantages, current critic-free RL frame-
works suffer from a severe credit assignment problem
[27, 35]. In standard GRPO, a single scalar advantage is
broadcast uniformly across an entire generated sequence,
which often spans thousands of tokens. This coarse as-
signment forces the model to treat pivotal reasoning break-
throughs and redundant transitional syntax with equal im-
portance, diluting the learning signal. Compounding this
inefficiency, the standard Kullback-Leibler (KL) penalty
[17, 49] constrains the policy to a reference model πref:
the unbounded reverse KL enforces extreme mode-seeking
conservatism, harshly penalizing any deviation from πref.
When the model explores novel, low-probability reasoning
trajectories, this unbounded penalty causes severe gradient
instability, disincentivizing innovative solutions and trap-
ping reasoning within the safe yet restricted bounds of the
pre-trained distribution [16].
To overcome these bottlenecks, we propose TIDE (Token-
level Importance via Deviation and Entropy), an RL frame-
work that reallocates coarse sequence-level advantages into
fine-grained, token-level update signals. TIDE is built on a
new perspective: deviation from the reference distribution
should not be uniformly penalized as an error, but interpreted
as a guiding signal for exploration. When a sequence yields
a positive reward, the tokens that diverge most from the refer-
ence model are often exactly the cognitive leaps responsible
for the success. To quantify this deviation safely, TIDE re-
places the reverse KL divergence with the bounded Hellinger
distance [1, 2, 37], which smoothly encourages exploratory
diversity without the risk of gradient explosion.

Relying solely on distribution deviation, however, risks
rewarding “fake innovations”: a large deviation may reflect
spurious or hallucinated tokens rather than a meaningful
reasoning step. When the model assigns high confidence
to an incorrect, out-of-distribution token, the resulting devi-
ation can be large despite lacking semantic validity. Such
confident hallucinations would be incorrectly reinforced by
deviation-based signals alone. TIDE therefore introduces an
entropy gating mechanism that scales the Hellinger distance
by the model’s normalized predictive uncertainty (entropy),
distinguishing deliberate, high-uncertainty exploration from
confident, low-entropy hallucinations. The gated scores then
dynamically redistribute the sequence-level advantage across
tokens: critical exploratory steps receive amplified gradients,
while standard syntactic tokens are naturally discounted.
We evaluate TIDE on the AIME 2024 and AIME 2025
mathematical reasoning benchmarks. Across model scales,
TIDE outperforms competitive critic-free alignment meth-
ods, achieving 60.0% Avg@32 accuracy on AIME 2024 and
46.0% on AIME 2025 with a Qwen2.5-32B backbone, above
strong baselines including DAPO [43]. Beyond outcome
accuracy, our computational profiling shows that TIDE pre-
serves the efficiency of standard GRPO: it obtains process-
level supervisory signals with only 3.6% time overhead and
a memory footprint comparable to vanilla GRPO, since it
reuses logits that GRPO already materializes for its KL term.
In summary, our contributions are three-fold: (i) We ana-
lyze the limitations of uniform sequence-level updates and
the unbounded reverse KL divergence in long-horizon tasks,
showing how they induce mode-seeking conservatism and
gradient instability. (ii) We propose a critic-free RL frame-
work that reinterprets distribution deviation as a guiding
signal: by combining the bounded Hellinger distance with
entropy gating, TIDE safely incentivizes critical exploratory
steps while filtering out confident hallucinations. (iii) We val-
idate TIDE across model scales (7B and 32B) on in-domain,
held-out math, and out-of-domain science and code bench-
marks with multi-seed confidence intervals and a paired
significance test, and further validate the mechanism itself
through token-level attribution, causal perturbation, and ex-
ploration analyses.

#### 2. Related Work

Reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning.
RL has
become the de facto standard for aligning LLMs with hu-
man intent and enhancing complex reasoning [9, 24]. While
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [30] initially dominated
RLHF, its reliance on an auxiliary value network imposes

prohibitive memory and computational overheads as param-
eters scale. This bottleneck has driven a paradigm shift
towards critic-free optimization [8, 9, 13, 14, 26, 43, 47].
Among these, GRPO [31] is exceptionally effective at elicit-
ing mathematical and logical reasoning, empowering models
to perform extensive CoT generation [48] by estimating the
baseline through group-wise reward normalization rather
than a parameterized critic. However, GRPO applies a uni-
form, sequence-level advantage to all tokens in a response
[20], treating every decoding step equally; in long-horizon
reasoning this inevitably obscures the learning signal and
slows convergence.
Fine-grained credit assignment.
The credit assignment
problem is a fundamental challenge in RL, uniquely exac-
erbated in autoregressive LLMs [27] where action spaces
span tens of thousands of tokens. To provide more granular
supervision, recent work has heavily investigated Process
Reward Models (PRMs) [21, 22, 32, 36, 42, 45] and im-
plicit or Monte-Carlo process rewards [5, 15], which assign
scalar rewards to intermediate reasoning steps, unlike Out-
come Reward Models [3, 4, 7, 41, 46] that evaluate only
the final answer. Despite their effectiveness, training robust
PRMs requires exhaustive human-annotated step-level data
or additional rollouts. Closer to our setting, several works
redistribute sequence-level rewards using attention weights
or heuristic decay mechanisms [19, 44], and recent studies
show that policy entropy itself marks decision-critical “fork-
ing” tokens [6, 39]. TIDE differs in that the reweighting sig-
nal is the joint product of entropy and the bounded Hellinger
deviation from the reference policy: our ablations show that
neither signal alone recovers the full gain, indicating that
the deviation carries information beyond entropy. TIDE
bypasses external PRMs and parameterized critics entirely:
by extracting inherent probabilistic deviations between the
current and reference policies, it establishes a self-contained
mechanism for token-level credit assignment.

#### 3. The TIDE Method


##### 3.1. Preliminaries and Motivation

In standard GRPO for CoT alignment, given a prompt x,
the policy πθ generates a group of candidate responses
{y1, . . . , yG}, which are evaluated to yield scalar rewards
{r1, . . . , rG}. The sequence-level advantage is computed by
group-wise Z-score normalization:
Ai = (ri −ˆµr) / (ˆσr + ε),

where ˆµr and ˆσr are the group mean and standard deviation
of the rewards. This paradigm suffers from two critical limi-
tations. First, Ai is distributed uniformly across all tokens of
a potentially long sequence, so the model cannot distinguish
pivotal reasoning breakthroughs from redundant transitional
phrases. Second, the objective incorporates an unbounded
KL penalty that constrains the policy to a reference model
πref: when the policy explores trajectories where πref as-
signs near-zero probability, the penalty can explode, leading
to severe gradient instability. TIDE addresses both issues by
reinterpreting distribution deviation not as a penalty but as a
guiding signal for critic-free, fine-grained credit assignment.

For the 32B model, we maintain a global batch size of

##### 4.2. Main Results

We benchmark TIDE against leading critic-free RL algo-
rithms, including standard GRPO and the highly competitive
DAPO. Figure 3 shows the validation accuracy on AIME
during training: TIDE improves steadily and converges to a
higher plateau than GRPO and DAPO without the early oscil-
lations of KL-penalized training. Table 1 summarizes perfor-
mance on the AIME benchmarks. On the 32B scale, TIDE
reaches 60.0% Avg@32 on AIME 2024, above the DAPO
baseline at 50.0%, and the consensus metric (Cons@32)
rises from 60.0% to 73.0%. On the more demanding AIME
2025, TIDE raises Avg@32 from 38.0% to 46.0%. Because
AIME is small and high-variance, we report bootstrap 95%
CIs (Table 1) and run a paired bootstrap significance test
between TIDE and the strongest baseline (FIPO); the im-
provement on Avg@32 is statistically significant (p < 0.02).
We note that some secondary metrics tie between TIDE and
FIPO (Cons@32 of 73.0 on AIME 2024 and Pass@32 of
67.0 on AIME 2025); our claims rest on Avg@32, where the
paired improvement is consistent across seeds.
The token-level credit assignment is further supported
by experiments on Qwen2.5-7B-Math (Table 2). Despite
the smaller scale, TIDE reaches 43.0% Avg@32 on AIME
2024, above vanilla GRPO (22.0%), DAPO (36.0%), and
FIPO (40.0%), indicating that localizing advantages with the
entropy-gated Hellinger distance extracts richer supervisory
signals from the same binary outcome rewards across model
scales.

##### 4.5. Validating the Gating Hypothesis

TIDE rests on the premise that the entropy-gated deviation
si,t = di,t · ˜Hκ
i,t (i) amplifies pivotal reasoning tokens and
(ii) suppresses confident, low-entropy hallucinations. We
validate this quantitatively on realistic training data. From
held-out successful CoT trajectories, we obtain ground-truth
“pivotal” token labels via two independent references: (1)
automatic annotation of pivotal reasoning steps by a strong
judge model, and (2) a counterfactual criterion, where a
token is pivotal if masking or resampling it and re-deriving
the solution flips the outcome. We then measure how well
each scoring signal identifies these tokens (Table 6).
Do amplified tokens matter causally?
Table 6 shows that
the gating score identifies pivotal tokens well above chance
and above the deviation-only signal. As a causal check, per-
turbing the top-5% amplified tokens degrades accuracy by
17.6 points, versus 3.2 points for a random 5%, confirm-
ing that these tokens are disproportionately responsible for
correct reasoning.
Are suppressed tokens really confident hallucinations?
Stratifying high-deviation tokens by entropy, low-entropy
ones are erroneous most of the time (67% error) and are
suppressed, whereas high-entropy ones are mostly valid ex-
plorations (23% error) and are amplified—supporting the
interpretation that the entropy gate, not deviation alone, sep-
arates hallucinations from exploration. Concrete CoT case
studies of amplified pivotal tokens and suppressed confi-
dent hallucinations, together with the full annotation and
counterfactual protocols, are provided in Appendix B.

#### 5. Conclusion

We introduced TIDE (Token-level Importance via Deviation
and Entropy), a critic-free RL framework that replaces the
unbounded reverse KL penalty with a bounded, entropy-
gated Hellinger distance for token-level credit reallocation.
TIDE outperforms strong baselines across the evaluated in-
domain, held-out, and out-of-domain benchmarks with neg-
ligible overhead. The principle—interpreting bounded dis-
tribution deviation, gated by predictive uncertainty, as a
fine-grained guidance signal—is promising for other long-
horizon decision-making settings for LLMs, including agen-
tic and multimodal reasoning, which we leave to future work.

#### A. Theoretical Analysis and Proofs

This section provides detailed derivations for the core mecha-
nisms of TIDE. Appendix A.1 demonstrates that the gradient
derived from the Hellinger distance remains strictly bounded
under extreme exploration scenarios where the reference
policy probability approaches zero, whereas the traditional
Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence leads to gradient explo-
sion. Appendix A.2 proves that TIDE’s reweighted gradi-
ent conserves the trajectory-level credit mass and remains
ascent-aligned (sign-consistent) with the vanilla policy gra-
dient. Appendix A.3 further shows, from the perspective of
stochastic approximation, that the entropy gating mechanism
preserves the fundamental convergence properties of policy
optimization.

##### A.3. Adaptive Step-Size Perspective of Entropy


##### Gating

The entropy gate can be read as a state-dependent, per-token
step-size modulator in the sense of stochastic approximation
[29]: since wi,t > 0, it rescales but never flips the sign of
the advantage, and its behavior bifurcates with the policy’s
predictive uncertainty:
• High-entropy states (deliberate exploration). When un-
certainty is high ( ˜Hi,t →1), wi,t is dominated by the
distribution deviation di,t. The gate allows amplified gra-
dients, accelerating learning at pivotal exploratory steps.
• Low-entropy states (confident hallucinations). If devia-
tion is high but uncertainty is low ( ˜Hi,t →0), the model
is likely generating a confident hallucination. The gate
compresses the joint score si,t →0, diminishing wi,t—an
adaptive decay of the effective step size at spurious or
overconfident tokens.
This is an interpretive account rather than a formal conver-
gence theorem; it explains why gating is compatible with
standard stochastic-approximation-based training, and no
global convergence claim is made.

#### B. Additional Experimental Results


##### B.1. Reweighting-Source Controls

To verify that TIDE’s gains stem specifically from the
entropy-gated distribution-guided signal, and not from any
non-uniform perturbation of the token-level optimization
dynamics, we hold the training pipeline fixed and only swap
the source of the redistribution weight wi,t: (a) uniform
weights (vanilla sequence-level credit); (b) random weights
matched to TIDE’s weight variance; (c) a position/length
heuristic (later tokens up-weighted); (d) sampled-token log-
ratio weights log(πθ/πref); (e) entropy-only weights; (f)
Hellinger-only weights; and (g) a KL-free high-entropy to-
ken weighting baseline in the style of Wang et al. [39], where
the top-20% highest-entropy tokens receive the full sequence
advantage and the remainder are down-weighted, applied on
top of the DAPO (KL-free) configuration. The results are
reported in Sec. 4.4. Random and position-based reweight-
ing recover no gain over the uniform baseline, ruling out
arbitrary non-uniformity as the cause. Entropy-only and
Hellinger-only weights each yield partial improvements, in-
dicating both signals are individually informative, but neither
matches the full method; only their gated product attains the
largest gain.

##### B.4. Protocol for the Gating-Hypothesis Validation

Sec. 4.5 compares scoring signals against ground-truth “piv-
otal” tokens. We document the full protocol here. Data. We
sample 500 successful long-horizon CoT trajectories gen-
erated by Qwen2.5-7B-Math on held-out prompts (disjoint
from DAPO-17K), covering 412,000 candidate tokens in
total. Reference 1 (judge annotation). A strong open-source
reasoning LLM of the 72B class is prompted with step-level
annotation guidelines to mark pivotal tokens (mathematical
substitutions, logical deductions, case splits); two annotation
passes with self-consistency filtering are used, and disagree-
ments are resolved by majority. Reference 2 (counterfactual).
A token is pivotal if masking or resampling it and re-deriving
the solution flips the outcome; each candidate token is per-
turbed with 8 resamples. Metrics. Precision@10% (fraction
of pivotal tokens among the top-10% scoring tokens), re-
call over pivotal tokens, and AUROC; the random baseline
reports the base rate of pivotal tokens. Error stratification.
The 67%/23% error rates are computed over high-deviation
tokens (di,t above the 80th percentile) split at the entropy
median, each stratum containing 9,600 tokens. Causal per-
turbation. Accuracy degradation from perturbing the top-5%
amplified tokens versus a random 5% is averaged over the
same 500-trajectory pool. All reported quantities are com-
puted over the full pool; bootstrap 95% CIs are included in
the released evaluation scripts.

##### B.6. Computational Profiling

The profiling results are reported in Sec. 4.6; measurements
are taken during alignment of Qwen2.5-7B-Math on 2 nodes
with 8×H20 (96 GB) GPUs using DeepSpeed ZeRO-3. PPO
imposes a prohibitive memory bottleneck (∼72.4 GB per
GPU) and low throughput due to the critic’s forward and
backward passes. GRPO and TIDE both operate at ∼46 GB
per GPU. Since TIDE reuses the same policy and reference
logits GRPO already materializes for its KL term to compute
the Hellinger distance and normalized entropy, its only over-
head is a set of parallelizable element-wise tensor operations,
yielding a marginal +3.6% time overhead with no additional

#### C. Limitations and Future Work


##### C.1. Limitations

While TIDE provides a robust and theoretically grounded
framework for fine-grained credit assignment, we acknowl-
edge several limitations.
Domain specificity. The empirical evaluation focuses
mainly on mathematical reasoning tasks, with AIME 2024
and AIME 2025 as the primary suites. While TIDE performs
well in eliciting mathematical CoT generation, its efficacy
in other alignment domains—such as open-ended creative
generation and general instruction-following—remains to be
validated more broadly.
Hyperparameter sensitivity. TIDE introduces two new
hyperparameters: the reallocation temperature τ and the
entropy gating factor κ. Although our sensitivity analysis
shows TIDE is stable within moderate ranges (e.g., τ ∈
[0.5, 1.0]), transitioning to entirely different tasks or model
architectures may still require empirical tuning to prevent
overly sparse updates or the accidental reinforcement of
confident hallucinations.
Evaluation variance and measurement scope. AIME
suites are small and high-variance; although we mitigate
this with multiple seeds and bootstrap confidence intervals,
and corroborate with additional held-out and out-of-domain
benchmarks, absolute margins on any single small suite
should still be read with care. The held-out and out-of-
domain results in Table 3 are computed from the primary
(single-seed) checkpoints per method; the multi-seed proto-
col is applied to the AIME suites. The sign-aware negative-
advantage branch (s−
i,t) is not separately ablated in this work,
and reporting all accuracies rounded to one decimal can
mask finer-grained seed-level variation; both are candidates
for a revised evaluation. Our gating-hypothesis validation
(Sec. 4.5) relies in part on judge-model and counterfactual
labels for “pivotal” tokens, which are approximate; the en-
tropy gate is a useful but imperfect separator, and a confident
low-entropy token can occasionally be a correct determin-
istic step rather than a hallucination, which is why TIDE
applies soft rather than hard suppression.
Reference-distribution computation. Computing the full-
vocabulary Hellinger distance requires the policy and refer-
ence distributions over the whole vocabulary at each token.
While this reuses logits GRPO already materializes for its
KL term and adds only marginal overhead in our setting,
methods that avoid full-vocabulary reference passes (e.g.,
sampled-token-only objectives) could be cheaper, and scal-
ing to very large vocabularies warrants further study.
Theoretical scope. Our analysis characterizes TIDE as a
bounded, preconditioned, sign-consistent policy gradient un-
der stop-gradient weighting (Assumptions A1–A3); it does
not establish unbiasedness, and the sufficient condition for
ascent alignment, while empirically satisfied, can in principle
be violated.

##### C.2. Future Work

Broader task generalization. Future work should evaluate
TIDE across a wider array of RL tasks for LLMs, includ-
ing multi-turn conversational alignment, general instruction
following, and complex agentic and multimodal workflows
where fine-grained credit assignment is equally critical.
Dynamic hyperparameter scheduling. Rather than static
values for τ and κ, future iterations could explore dynamic
scheduling or adaptive, state-dependent mechanisms, e.g.,
automatically decaying these parameters based on the global
training step or the moving average of the sequence-level
advantage, to further stabilize training and eliminate manual
tuning.
