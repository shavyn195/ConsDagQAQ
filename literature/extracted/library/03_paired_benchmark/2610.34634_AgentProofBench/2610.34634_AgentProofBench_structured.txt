#### ABSTRACT

Reusable Web-agent verification requires a rule to follow chang-
ing requirements and support subsequent execution. We introduce
AgentProofBench, which connects paired tests of a frozen accep-
tance rule to execution from identical saved openings. The resource
spans 97 tasks, 79 families, and four Web services. Experiments with
DeepSeek Flash/Pro establish two linked measurements. Across 48
families, complete-panel and decision-replay tests retain 82/96 Flash
and 79/96 Pro source programs; paired reuse–rebuild BA intervals
are [−5.47, 1.57] and [−10.16, 0.00] percentage points. Across 22
execution families, deployment-state panel correctness separates
program assignments whose two saved openings are both delivered
correctly: 32/33 assignments with error-free panels, versus 1/11
with panel errors. All reference-defined program-gate regressions
and withheld deliveries occur in the latter group. Neutral continua-
tion attains the highest correct delivery under the tested generic
feedback, providing a concrete baseline for measuring the value
added by a gate. Released instructions, states, programs, reference

#### 1


#### INTRODUCTION

Reusable acceptance checks can make Web-agent operation fast and
inspectable. A program can encode the conditions for completing a
task, inspect the relevant business state, and issue the same explicit
checks on subsequent executions. The central challenge is that the
public instruction can stay fixed while the required work changes.
The current owner of a name, the files belonging to a collection,
or the value that must be preserved comes from the initial state of
each execution. A verifier that remembers an earlier answer can
produce a convincing decision for the wrong requirement.
The decision also becomes part of the agent’s environment. Re-
jection can trigger further edits, and acceptance can release the
resulting state. Evaluating a reusable verifier therefore requires a
link between the conditions its tests exercise and the outcomes of

the policy that consumes its decisions. Matched continuation histo-

preserved, changed, or withheld under different stopping policies.
Existing Web-agent environments establish executable task eval-
uation [15, 20, 22, 23, 31]. Work on automatic assessment and gen-
erated verifiers demonstrates the importance of evidence and accep-
tance of valid alternatives [8, 16, 24]. Reusable execution policies
further show the value of experience across workflow instances [19].
These advances motivate an evaluation that connects three proper-
ties of an acceptance rule. It must recognize the permitted terminal
outcomes, recover the requirements of a changed initial state, and
support effective continuation and delivery decisions.
We introduce AgentProofBench around this connection. An ac-
ceptance program is executable code that judges task completion
through ordinary before/after APIs. A reuse contract fixes the public
instruction and evidence permissions while initial business data
vary. Matched terminal outcomes make the consequences of this
variation observable. Shared actor openings then connect the same
verifier to the actions taken after its decision. Every measurement
is linked to versioned reference predicates, program source, API
evidence, and a reconstructable business state.
The benchmark provides three contributions.
• A paired reuse test that holds the public instruction and
generated program fixed while changing its required initial-
state bindings. Complete outcome panels and replayed de-
cisions measure complementary obligations.
• An execution-validity test linking deployment-state panel
correctness to matched policy outcomes. The 22-family
study preserves 44 program assignments, two openings per
assignment, and 352 policy outcomes.
• An executable resource with 97 tasks across four Web ser-
vices, including versioned reference rules, legal alternatives,
reconstructable states, programs, and action histories.
The central empirical result is the connection between tests
and execution. Programs with error-free deployment-state panels
correctly deliver both saved openings in 32 of 33 assignments, com-
pared with one of eleven assignments whose panels contain errors.
The paired reuse study explains which requirements those panels
exercise; replay witnesses expose additional errors in three source
programs. Fixed-instance acceptance and cost profiles then describe
the operating tradeoffs of the same verification interface. The re-
ported generator, judge, and actor results use DeepSeek Flash/Pro
with thinking disabled.

#### RELATED WORK

Executable evaluation and generated verifiers. WebArena, OS-
World, 𝜏-bench, and AppWorld provide executable task environ-
ments [20, 22, 23, 31]. Agent-Diff supplies the enterprise-service
replicas used here [15]; OpenComputer and WorkForge develop
verifiable software worlds and workspace criteria [21, 27]. The test-
oracle literature establishes the broader problem of trustworthy
acceptance rules [1]. CodeT and Eureka generate executable tests
or rewards [3, 9], while SpecGuardBench and WebGrader evaluate
generated validation code [2, 30]. Rules to Tools supplies callable
implementations of scientific requirements for program repair [12].
VerifierBench tests executable verifier generation across six agent
benchmarks, including legal alternatives, ordinary errors, and at-
tacks [24]. AgentProofBench extends the measurement target to
matched initial-state requirements and the subsequent execution
controlled by verifier decisions.
Evidence-based judgment. Agent-as-a-Judge, AJ-Bench, and
AgentRewardBench study assessment from environment and
trajectory evidence [8, 18, 32]. Universal Verifier develops criterion-
based computer-use assessment [16]; Sauron gathers state and
trace evidence for its judgments [17]. VAGEN and VeriHarness
study agentic verification and structured evidence collection [4, 26].
LLM-as-a-Verifier examines continuous scores, repeated evaluation,
and criterion decomposition [7]. Our fresh API judgments share the
frozen states and ordinary evidence permissions of the executable
programs, enabling direct comparison of valid acceptance, failure
rejection, and repeated-use cost.
Reuse and verification during execution. SkillLearnBench eval-
uates learned artifacts across task instances [29]. ControlScope
studies how workflow-revision permissions shape subsequent ex-
ecution [10]. Revision or Re-Solving uses matched conditions to
separate sources of second-pass gains [11]. CLIFT reuses a frozen
self-verification bank for training and trajectory selection [28].
Neuro-Symbolic Computer Use learns reusable execution policies
under initial-state and parameter variation, with verification-guided
continuation [19]. SPLIT allocates computation across sampling
and imperfect verification, and verifier hardening combines ad-
versarial improvement with protection of valid solutions [6, 25].
Enterprise-agent studies connect verification, model roles, and task
completion [5]. The Implementation Lottery compares reuse of a
selected program with fresh implementations of its mechanism [14].
DCP compares truthful and neutral feedback from shared check-
points in scientific-research audits [13]. Figure 2 connects paired
verifier tests with execution from shared histories.

#### 3.2


#### Outcome coverage and reference semantics

Three linked studies address acceptance, reuse, and execution. The
acceptance resource contains 97 tasks from 79 families, with 20
family-disjoint development tasks. Its 556-state natural panel in-
cludes 452 successes and 104 failures, sampled by reference class
with at most six states per task. The reuse study evaluates 576
programs on 558 terminal states from 48 families. The execution
study compares policies on 88 saved openings from 22 families.
Appendix A records the natural-state selection flow and the three
post-inspection task exclusions.
Four outcome panels separate common acceptance demands (Fig-
ure 7). Natural outcomes measure ordinary agent endings. Native-
prefix pairs use service actions to connect a complete execution
to an earlier incomplete state. Typed faults alter a successful an-
chor through service APIs, allowing the violated requirement to
be identified. Legal alternatives change behavior that the public
instruction permits. Successful anchors and failed states jointly test
valid acceptance and failure rejection.
Reference predicates cover identities, values, collections, and
relationships. Versioned selectors recover the required objects from
each before state. Native-state and ordinary-API evaluation share
the predicates and check restoration and evidence accessibility.
Scores measure conformity to this explicit reference contract. Ap-
pendix A retains the original and explicit-preservation label views.
A separate GPT-6 Astra LLM-as-a-judge audit reviews 195 states
across 62 tasks, with reference verdicts withheld during initial
judgment. It agrees with 191 of 192 resolved labels (99.5%); three
cases are indeterminate. The audit corroborates all 48 requirement
changes, rejects all 48 failed replays, and confirms both delivered-
regression pairs (Appendix A).
The preservation task linear_52 illustrates the contract. Its
instruction assigns Plot B3 to Rashida and requires its initial sta-
tus to survive. The source state has status Active; the related
state has Dormant. The reference resolves Plot B3 from the cur-
rent before state, checks the requested assignee, and compares its
before/after status identifiers. A correct related outcome therefore
keeps Dormant. The instruction’s other clauses create a tracker
issue and update Plots A7 and C1. They supply additional matched
outcomes while the B3 change isolates the state-dependent preser-
vation requirement. The complete instruction and predicates appear
in Appendix A.

#### 3.3


#### Matched initial-state reuse

The matched study, P1, selects one task from each of 48 original fam-
ilies, comprising 18 Linear, 10 Slack, 19 Box, and one Calendar task.
Every family contributes one related initial-state transformation.
Each public instruction has three initial conditions. Source is the
construction state. Background changes data outside the requested
work. Related changes a required entity binding, initial value, col-
lection, or preservation condition. An entity binding identifies the
object denoted by a public name; a preservation condition requires
an initial value to survive execution.
Qualification establishes four properties before prediction. The
changed requirement follows the public instruction, service actions
construct the states, native and API references agree on matched
outcomes, and replaying a source-valid decision becomes invalid
under the related condition. This last execution is a source-decision
witness. It tests the acceptance rule against an earlier valid business
decision whose required target or value has changed.
The witness gives initial-state variation a direct semantic test. If
the same earlier decision remains valid, a program could succeed
by retaining its source answer. A qualified witness requires the ver-
ifier to resolve a new condition from the current before state. This
separates state-dependent acceptance from incidental differences
in a snapshot and makes the changed obligation inspectable in the
reference and API trace.
Each initial condition contains a complete result, a distinct legal
alternative where available, and two changed failures. Fault recipes
and their roles remain matched across conditions; the current be-
fore state supplies identifiers and required values. Forty-two tasks
have two valid outcomes per condition, and six have one. The 144
initial states support 558 main terminal states, comprising 270 valid
and 288 failed entries. Witness pairs add one source-valid and one
related-invalid decision per task, with their own scoring support.
DeepSeek Flash and Pro, both with thinking disabled, each gen-
erate two source programs and two rebuilt programs at each tar-
get condition, producing 576 frozen artifacts. Construction and

#### 3.4


#### Verification during continued execution

The execution study, P2, uses 22 families with a native completion
trace containing at least two mutating operations. One Flash and
one Pro actor per task generate 44 trajectories from related initial
states. Every trajectory supplies a natural ending and a closed
checkpoint, saved after its first complete tool round containing a
mutating request. Each of the 88 openings preserves the original
initial state, current business state, and complete interaction history.
Four policies branch from each opening. Actor stop delivers on
the actor’s final reply. Its non-final checkpoint outcome is a me-
chanical no-delivery reference. Fixed continue allows two neutral
continuation phases and delivers on a final reply after phase two.
Program gate uses a source-built Pro program, assigned by a fixed
parity rule per task and actor independently of source quality. On-
line gate uses fresh Pro API12 judgments. Both gates deliver on Pass
and otherwise permit at most two continuations. The two cohorts
retain the same assigned program.
The neutral message asks the actor to finish the original re-
quest while preserving completed work. Gate feedback prepends
a statement of nonacceptance and carries no program checks or
entity identifiers. Thus each branch evaluates a complete feedback-
and-stopping policy; Appendix C gives the exact messages. An
eight-family development study selected this design before formal
actor prediction. Each actor phase allows 20 model turns and 100
business operations, with a USD 1 observed-cost stop and a 900-
second deadline. Unresolved usage stops further work. Executed
writes persist when delivery is withheld.

#### 4.1


#### Joint tests of initial-state reuse

The paired study measures whether an acceptance rule follows a
changed requirement while its instruction and code remain fixed.
Source programs obtain related-state BA of 93.23% for Flash and
90.36% for Pro (Table 1). Reuse-minus-rebuild estimates are -1.56
points with interval [-5.47, 1.57] and -4.95 with [-10.16, 0.00]. Both
prespecified intervals include equal mean BA; the Pro range ex-
tends to −10.16 points. The background control produces identical
verdicts under the tested unrelated edits.
Complete main-panel correctness changes from 89/96 to 83/96
source programs for Flash and from 83/96 to 81/96 for Pro. Con-
ditional on a fully correct source panel, the retained counts are
83/89 and 80/83, spanning 45 and 46 source-qualified families. Six
Flash and three Pro programs acquire an error across three families
per model; one initially incorrect Pro program becomes correct on
the related panel. Figure 3 keeps the full 96-program denominator
visible.
The added witness probes an earlier valid decision at its changed
requirement. Source programs accept 92/96 valid source witnesses
for Flash and 89/96 for Pro. After the related change, they falsely
accept 6/96 and 5/96 replays, while related rebuilds accept 5/96 and
2/96. Pro source programs also give two Unknown and two Error
verdicts on related witnesses. Joint correctness requires a resolved,
correct verdict on both the main panel and its witness. Its resulting
counts are 82/96 Flash and 79/96 Pro source programs, compared
with 82/96 and 86/96 rebuilds (Figure 3). Rebuilt main panels are
completely correct for 85/96 Flash and 87/96 Pro programs. Within
the source-joint-qualified subsets, related joint correctness is 82/89
and 78/83.

#### 4.2


#### Panel correctness and execution outcomes

Deployment-state tests are strongly associated with the recorded
execution outcomes. Of 33 assigned programs with error-free re-
lated panels, 32 correctly deliver both their natural ending and
checkpoint. Of eleven assignments with a panel error, one cor-

corresponding counts are 64/66 and 2/22. The assignment is the
summary unit: its two openings share one trajectory and have iden-
tical correct-versus-incorrect/withheld dispositions in this study.
The 44 assignments come from 22 task families. The associated
correct-delivery difference is 87.9 percentage points, with a point-
wise family-cluster interval of [68.8, 100.0].

defined regressions belong to the panel-error group. Thirteen with-
held terminal states satisfy the reference, so withholding and refer-

#### Construction cost and the reuse horizon

Repeated use separates the fixed cost of creating a program from
the cost of invoking it. The matched P1 fee comparison contains

with known charges for all three constructions and their online
judgments. Median source construction costs are USD 0.0173/0.0765.

fee ratio upward gives median API-fee break-even horizons of
twelve assessments for Flash and ten for Pro, with ranges 2–29 and
2–36.

actor-prefix and branch cost. The amortized fee at 𝑘uses is 𝑐+

The reuse horizon complements the acceptance and delivery
profiles. A low-cost invocation is useful when its rule covers the
required outcomes, and a highly discriminative judge can justify
repeated inference where valid completions vary. The observed
policy branches further show that construction amortization and
downstream correctness are separate measurements. The released
ledger counts each request once; policy views allocate the shared
prefix and assigned construction explicitly. The companion retains
every cold, warm, and amortized policy record.

#### 5


#### USING THE BENCHMARK

AgentProofBench supports three concrete evaluation workflows. A
verifier generator can be tested on the same natural endings, legal
alternatives, and typed failures to establish its acceptance profile.
Its frozen outputs can then be evaluated under matched initial-state
requirements using both complete-panel and joint witness correct-
ness. Finally, the recorded actor openings support comparison of
the policies that consume these decisions, measuring completed
work, persistent mutations, and delivered state separately.
The resource connects these workflows through stable task, fam-
ily, program, and state identities. Each false acceptance links to a
violated reference condition; each transfer error links to a source
program and target rebuild; each execution outcome links to the
gate decision and subsequent requests. A generator improvement
can therefore be assessed against the changed requirement it should
recover and the policy outcome it should support.
The companion’s Evidence Index provides the acceptance, reuse,
and execution entry points. Canonical snapshots and exact row
deltas reconstruct the saved states. The verify(task, before,
after) interface evaluates frozen programs without model calls.

#### 6


#### CONCLUSION

AgentProofBench connects the two requirements of reusable
Web-agent verification: recovering the current task obligation and
supporting subsequent execution. Paired initial states and replay
witnesses test the first; shared actor histories and program-level
outcome links test the second. In the DeepSeek Flash/Pro study,
deployment-state panel correctness identifies 32/33 assignments
whose two openings are delivered correctly, compared with 1/11
assignments containing panel errors. Neutral continuation anchors
the measurement of gate value, while acceptance profiles and
amortized costs describe operating choices. The released 97-task re-
source makes this chain reproducible, from a changed requirement
to a tested rule and its observed execution consequences.

intervals; Holm-adjusted 𝑝values are 0.4350 for Flash and 0.1254

exploratory analysis of frozen assignments. Its bootstrap resamples
22 task families, retaining both actor assignments and both open-
ings; 9,999 of 10,000 draws contain both panel groups. The statistic
is the difference in the fraction of assignments whose two openings
are both delivered correctly.

#### CONSTRUCTION AND ARCHIVES

In the original construction study, SelfReview uses the instruction
and documentation, InitialRead adds initial-state API reads, and
Bounded adds execution feedback. B4 and B12 denote four- and
twelve-call construction budgets. InitialRead minus SelfReview im-
proves natural BA by 12.0 [4.2, 20.4] points for Flash and 12.3 [4.2,
20.8] for Pro. The resource retains the thinking-mode, construction-
budget, and workflow comparisons with their full intervals.
The source diagnosis inspects 149 false rejections from 22 pro-
grams: 109 representation/identifier cases, eight omitted legal alter-
natives, seven exact-match constraints, nine unrequested preserva-
tion conditions, and sixteen other task-binding errors. Rule-specific
edits assisted by gpt-6.1-sol change all 149 inspected decisions
to acceptance. The eighteen available negative controls return Fail
afterward, compared with seventeen beforehand; four affected pro-
grams have no eligible control. This diagnostic evaluates the edits
on the inspected cases and available negative controls; replay uses
the saved artifacts. All benchmark scores use the original frozen
predictions.

matched four-state target panel, a separate two-state source probe,

quests. The formal experiment uses its own family cohort, matched
three-condition panels, and saved checkpoint branches.
