1.10. Remark. Earlier versions of Theorems 1.2 and 1.8 for classical Lp spaces can be found
in [10]. The literature on the factorization of Muckenhoupt weights in the constant-exponent
setting has since expanded considerably; see for example, the works by Cao–Olivo–Yabuta [2],
Hytönen–Lappas [9], Liu–Wu–Yang [12], Lorist-Nieraeth [13] and Martín-Reyes–Rivera-Ríos [14].
1.11. Remark. Theorems 1.4 and 1.9 have their predecessors for constant exponent Lp spaces in
[10]. We observe the following differences. In the constant exponent case, the initial boundedness
assumption is made for some exponent and all weights, while Theorems 1.4 and 1.9 make the
initial assumption for all exponents (in a certain range) and all weights. In the constant exponent
case, the boundedness with some exponent (and all weights) implies the boundedness for all
exponents (and all weights) by the classical Rubio de Francia extrapolation of boundedness [17].
Versions of Rubio de Francia’s extrapolation for variable-exponent spaces are due to Cruz-Uribe–
Wang [5] (with further extensions by Cao–Marín–Martell [1] and Nieraeth [16]).
However, in their results, the starting point of the extrapolation is boundedness for some
constant exponent (and all weights), and variable exponents only appear in the conclusions.
Such results would allow one to replace the boundedness assumption for all variable exponents
(p0(·), q0(·)) by some constant exponents (p0, q0) in the same class, but it seems open whether
it is possible to start from the boundedness for just one non-constant variable exponent pair
(p0(·), q0(·)). We do not seem to have an analogue of Rubio de Francia’s extrapolation theorem
on weighted variable exponent Lebesgue spaces. Furthermore, we point out that Cruz-Uribe–
Wang (see [5, Theorem 2.7]) proved that operators satisfying the hypotheses of the extrapolation
theorem for Muckenhoupt Ap weights are bounded on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces. Similar
results hold in the off-diagonal and limited range setting (see [5, Theorems 2.11 and 2.14]). For
further extensions of [5] we refer to the recent works by Cao–Marín–Martell [1] and Nieraeth
[16].
