# SCHEDBench review

Source: [ACL Anthology 2026.findings-emnlp.852](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.852/), *SCHEDBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating LLM Constraint Faithfulness in Natural-Language Combinatorial Scheduling*. The ACL Anthology proceedings record and Crossref DOI `10.18653/2026.findings-emnlp.852` were checked on 2026-11-24 UTC. The local PDF and extracted text are recorded in `source_verification.json`.

SCHEDBench evaluates 1,132 canonical scheduling instances from six source families. It varies domain framing, entity names, templates, paraphrases, and constraint presentation, while a solver supplies feasibility and objective checks. The authors evaluate 13 frontier and open-weight models. Two expert annotators independently verify a stratified sample of 100 instances across all six families and three difficulty tiers, then jointly reconcile discrepancies; the paper reports zero semantic errors in this audited sample.

This is a direct prior for constraint faithfulness under equivalent natural-language surface forms and solver-checked outcomes. It rules out claims that paired language variation, surface-form robustness, or executable constraint checking is itself new. Its independent expert audit is also a stronger label-validity reference than ConsDAG's generator-only witness checks. The task differs from ConsDAG's one-request compiler interface: SCHEDBench asks for a complete scheduling solution and does not jointly score a typed constraint record consumed by a fixed downstream planner or separate explicit, implicit, and none renderings. Any ConsDAG contribution must be stated at that narrower interface and evaluation-protocol level, and its generator-defined semantic labels remain independently unvalidated.
