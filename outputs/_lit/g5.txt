15:Modern large language models (LLMs) are expected not just to answer correctly, but to adapt their
16:behavior to different human values and use cases. As a result, multi-reward reinforcement learning (RL)
17:has become an increasingly important problem for LLMs, where each reward captures a different aspect
18:of desired behavior. However, optimizing with multiple rewards suffers from a more severe alignment
19:tax issue, where different optimization objectives can trade off or even conflict with each other, leading
20:to unstable and inefficient post-training. In this work, we propose PRISM , a new multi-reward RL
21:framework built upon the idea of policy-space decomposition and composition. Instead of compositing
22:different rewards, PRISM optimizes a set of standalone positive policies and a global negative
23:policy. This alleviates the potential conflict during multi-reward policy optimization, while enabling
24:controllability during inference by flexible policy composition. Experiments on scientific reasoning,
25:tool-use reasoning, and helpfulness–safety alignment show that PRISM consistently outperforms

29:Introduction
30:Large language models [2, 19] are widely deployed as general-purpose assistants, making alignment with
31:human intent a central concern [4, 13]. Reinforcement learning [35] is among the most effective tools for this
32:purpose, optimizing LLMs directly against reward signals that encode human preferences [5, 23]. Yet human
33:preferences are rarely captured by a single objective: users expect models to be helpful, safe, and honest at
34:the same time [21, 39], and these goals often conflict, so improving one dimension costs another [18, 23, 42].
--INTRO END--
54:
55:multi-reward alignment tax.
56:We therefore introduce PRISM , a multi-reward RL algorithm built on a different philosophy: do not mix
57:rewards, mix policies. As shown in Figure 1, PRISM learns one standalone positive policy per reward and
58:a single global negative policy. Desirable behaviors are reward-specific and deserve dedicated optimization
59:directions. In contrast, undesirable behaviors need not be attributed to individual rewards. A single negative
60:policy captures the union of all reward-specific failure modes and penalizes them jointly. Each reward drives
61:updates mainly through its own positive branch. This design alleviates gradient competition among conflicting
62:preferences and mitigates the multi-reward alignment tax.
63:For efficiency, all sub-policies share a single language model conditioned on different prefix tokens and are
64:composed at sampling time via a weighted sum of logits; the same mixture policy serves both training rollouts
65:and inference. The merge weights thus form an explicit, interpretable interface for preference trade-offs,
66:adjustable at inference time without retraining.
67:Experiments on three multi-reward settings show that PRISM consistently outperforms reward-space baselines.
68:On GPQA [30] and ScienceQA [32], it achieves the best overall score on every backbone, surpassing the
69:strongest baseline by 17.8, 8.0, and 0.6 points on DeepSeek-R1-1.5B [34], Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-
70:3B-Instruct [37].
71:On the tool-calling benchmark BFCL-v3 [24] and the helpfulness–safety benchmarks
72:Alpaca [36], HH-RLHF [10], and PKU-SafeRLHF [6], it ranks first among all RL methods, and remains more
73:robust and controllable as reward complexity grows.
74:Our contributions are threefold:
75:• We identify the multi-reward alignment tax induced by reward-space composition, and advocate composing
76:preferences in policy space instead.
77:• We propose PRISM , which decomposes multi-reward RL into per-reward positive policies and a global
78:negative policy, realized via prefix-conditioned sub-policies with logit-level composition.
79:• Experiments on scientific QA, tool-use reasoning, and helpfulness–safety alignment show consistent gains
80:over strong baselines, with training-free inference-time controllability.

--LIMITATIONS--
1696:Limitations
1697:There are several limitations in this study. First, PRISM assumes that each reward can be represented by a
1698:distinct positive policy. It also assumes that a single negative policy can capture the union of reward-specific
1699:failure modes. This design is effective across all our experimental settings, which cover up to three rewards.
1700:Validating it at substantially larger reward counts is left for future work. Second, the current policy-space
1701:composition relies on manually specified merge weights, and how to automatically adapt these weights to
1702:different prompts, users, or deployment contexts remains underexplored. Third, although per-token latency
1703:stays that of a single forward pass, mixture decoding requires multiple prefix-conditioned branches, and
1704:its memory and FLOPs scale with the number of rewards. Future work can study more adaptive policy
1705:composition mechanisms, more expressive negative-policy designs, and more efficient decoding strategies.

--CONCLUSION--
1122:Conclusion
1123:In this work, we propose PRISM , a policy-space composition framework for multi-reward LLM alignment.
1124:Instead of scalarizing heterogeneous rewards into a single optimization signal, PRISM keeps each reward
1125:as a separate optimization direction by learning one positive policy per reward. A single global negative
1126:policy further captures the union of failure modes. These policies are composed through explicit merge
1127:weights, enabling controllable preference trade-offs without retraining. Experiments on scientific reasoning,
1128:tool-use reasoning, and helpfulness–safety alignment show that PRISM consistently outperforms reward-space
1129:baselines, remains more robust as reward complexity increases, and supports effective inference-time preference
1130:control. These results suggest that mixing policies rather than rewards offers a more effective and controllable
1131:path toward multi-reward aligned language models.

15:Abstract
16:Reinforcement learning fine-tuning of large
17:language models increasingly adopts multi-
18:ple reward dimensions, including verifiable
19:rules, task-specific evaluators, and learned re-
20:ward models, to provide richer supervision
21:across diverse capabilities. These dimensions
22:are commonly scalarized with fixed aggrega-
23:tion weights. We identify a failure mode in
24:which aggregation itself induces reward hack-
25:ing: static projection aliases qualitatively differ-
26:ent reward profiles into a single scalar, steering
27:optimization toward whichever dimensions are
28:easiest, densest, or systematically favored by
29:the reward signal. Over training, this traps the
30:policy in suboptimal profiles and prevents con-
31:vergence to better-balanced ones that would
32:yield higher task performance. To address this,
33:we propose Adaptive Multi-Reward Projection
34:(AMRP), a lightweight online method that re-
35:allocates aggregation weights using three sig-
36:nals—relative shortfall, reward volatility, and
37:recent progress—increasing pressure on lag-
38:ging, unstable, or stagnant dimensions while
39:relieving saturated ones.
40:Across structured
41:reasoning, citation-grounded generation, and
42:open-ended alignment under GRPO, AMRP
43:consistently improves reward-profile balance
44:and downstream performance over fixed and
45:dynamic weighting baselines; it also remains
46:effective with GDPO and PPO, supporting
47:compatibility across RL algorithms.
