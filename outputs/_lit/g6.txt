13:Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) treats all constraint violations equally: a $1 budget overshoot
14:and a $1,000 overshoot induce the same training signal. It is also susceptible to length and style
15:bias when preference pairs come from different model families. We introduce Constraint-Margin

16:ABSTRACT
17:Verifiable instruction-following benchmarks emphasize output-constraint satisfac-
18:tion but can overlook whether responses meaningfully answer the underlying re-
19:quest, creating an evaluation blind spot. We show that frontier models can re-
20:ceive full credit for responses with little substantive answer content. Using the
21:same verifier-based criterion as a training reward can induce reward hacking, as
22:publicly released checkpoints exhibit higher constraint satisfaction but lower re-
23:sponse quality after reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. To address
24:this reward hacking, we introduce CALIF, a lightweight method that augments
25:verifier rewards with a calibrated likelihood-based bonus to preserve response
26:quality. CALIF evaluates response likelihood under the request alone using the
27:existing frozen reference policy, requiring no additional judge or learned reward
28:model. Across models ranging from 1.7B to 8B in reasoning and non-reasoning
29:modes, CALIF improves constraint satisfaction on IFBench while maintaining
30:response quality near or above pre-RL levels. It also outperforms correspond-
31:ing base policies in 40 of 45 model–metric comparisons across three rubric-based
32:instruction-following benchmarks, demonstrating gains beyond training verifiers.

34:INTRODUCTION
35:Instruction following is central to the practical use of large language models (LLMs), enabling them
36:to translate user intent into desired outputs (Ouyang et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Chung et al.,
37:2024). In real-world applications, a user input typically comprises a request, which specifies the
38:task to be performed, and one or more constraints, which define the conditions the response must
39:satisfy (Jiang et al., 2024; Wen et al., 2024; He et al., 2024; 2026). Successful instruction following
40:therefore requires both high Response Quality by providing a substantive answer to the request
41:and high Constraint Satisfaction by adhering to the stated constraints. However, achieving both
42:objectives consistently remains challenging even for highly capable models (Zhang et al., 2025).
43:Evaluating instruction following for open-ended user inputs heavily relies on human assessment or
44:LLM judgments, which can be costly, subjective, and sensitive to the choice of evaluator (Zheng
45:et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Thakur et al., 2025). Verifiable instruction-following
46:(VIF) benchmarks (Zhou et al., 2023; He et al., 2024) address these evaluation challenges by fo-
47:cusing on requirements that can be checked programmatically. IFBench (Pyatkin et al., 2025), for
48:example, appends output constraints to a request and measures Constraint Satisfaction using pro-
49:grammatic verifiers, enabling scalable and reproducible evaluation without relying on LLM judges.

14:Abstract
15:LLM agents that execute multi-step tool calls must satisfy two objectives simul-
16:taneously: completing the user’s task (utility) and conforming to domain policies
17:and correctness constraints (safety). These objectives are in tension – blocking an
18:unsafe action prevents a violation but can leave the agent stranded with no prin-
19:cipled way to recover. Existing approaches sacrifice one objective for the other.
20:We introduce SOTERIA, a framework that reconciles safety and utility through
21:verified hierarchical planning and runtime enforcement of specifications. Before
22:execution, the agent generates a structured plan that is formally verified against
23:the specifications prior to any tool invocation. During execution, the verified plan
24:serves dual roles: it ensures that the agent takes specification-conformant trajec-
25:tories and provides guidance when unsafe actions are blocked. Across multiple
26:benchmarks covering a diverse range of tool-use tasks, SOTERIA achieves 1.7
27:times the average task success rate of the best baselines, with no violations of
28:policy specifications, demonstrating that safety and utility need not be traded off.

1003:Conclusion
1004:We presented SOTERIA, a framework that enables verification and runtime enforcement of plans for
1005:LLM-based agentic systems. By representing plans as guarded transition systems with hierarchical
1006:sub-plans, SOTERIA formally verifies LLM-generated plans against policy specifications, covering
1007:temporal, relational, and value-flow constraints, before any tool is invoked. At runtime, the veri-
1008:fied plan guides the executor, while the SpecMonitor blocks specification violations and the plan
1009:provides recovery guidance. Our evaluation shows that SOTERIA achieves 100% conformance with
1010:zero specification violations across all models, while improving task success by 4.7–43.9 percentage
1011:points over the best baseline.
1012:Limitations and future work. First, SOTERIA requires a set of formalized specifications as inputs,
1013:which are currently obtained through a semi-automated workflow that combines LLM-based auto-
1014:formalization with manual validation. In future work we aim to investigate how auto-formalization
1015:(e.g., An et al. [2026]) could help reduce this manual effort. Second, SOTERIA’s plan verification
1016:assumes that tool contracts faithfully model tool behavior and postconditions are satisfied at run-
1017:time. Furthermore, SOTERIA leverages automated theorem provers Leino [2010], De Moura and
1018:Bjørner [2008] that support limited first-order theories and may not complete verification in prac-
