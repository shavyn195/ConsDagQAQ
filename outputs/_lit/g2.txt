15:Abstract
16:Planning for real-world problems by language models often involves both
17:world and user constraints, which may not be fully specified upfront and
18:are progressively disclosed through interaction. However, existing bench-
19:marks still underexplore adaptive planning under such progressively re-
20:vealed dual constraints. To address this gap, we introduce AdaPlanBench,
21:a dynamic interactive benchmark for evaluating whether Large Language
22:Model (LLM) agents can adaptively plan and re-plan under progressively
23:revealed world and user constraints. AdaPlanBench is built on 307 house-
24:hold tasks, with a scalable constraint construction pipeline that augments
25:each task with dual constraints. At runtime, agents interact with the en-
26:vironment in a multi-turn protocol where hidden constraints are revealed
27:only when the agent proposes a plan that violates them, requiring iterative
28:plan revision under accumulating feedback. This makes planning chal-
29:lenging, as agents must infer and track constraints from feedback while
30:re-planning effectively. Experiments on ten leading LLMs show that adap-
31:tive planning under dual constraints remains challenging, with the best
32:model reaching only 67.75% accuracy. We further observe that perfor-
33:mance degrades as more constraints accumulate, with user constraints
34:posing a particularly large challenge and failures often stemming from
35:weaker physical grounding and reduced effectiveness. These results estab-
36:lish AdaPlanBench as a testbed for dual-constrained interactive planning
37:and highlight the challenge of reliable adaptation to dynamically revealed
38:constraints in LLM agents.

659:challenging for all evaluated models. Even GPT-5, the best-performing model, reaches only

669:models, affecting both consistent constraint adherence and overall plan quality.

671:A high valid
672:plan ratio (VPR) does not guarantee strong end-task accuracy. For example, both Gemini-3.1-
673:Pro and Gemini-3-Flash achieve relatively high VPRs, exceeding 90%, yet their accuracies
674:remain below 45%. This pattern is further supported by their relatively low AWRV and

11:Abstract
12:Neural combinatorial optimization (NCO) rapidly produces
13:high-quality solutions for problem structures encountered dur-
14:ing training, but operational requirements may change af-
15:ter deployment and be communicated in natural language.
16:We propose LangNCO, a framework that compiles instance-
17:specific requirements into structured inference-time interven-
18:tions for a frozen NCO solver, without changing the pre-
19:trained policy’s architecture, learned input representation, or
20:parameters. A pretrained LLM serves as a replaceable se-
21:mantic front end, translating each requirement into a schema-
22:validated domain-specific language (DSL) rule set. A deter-
23:ministic Rule Executor applies the rules as nonexpansive hard
24:action masks. Because local masking prevents immediate vi-
25:olations but does not ensure that a complete feasible contin-
26:uation remains reachable, Critical-Offset Decoding extracts
27:rule-referenced critical nodes and reallocates part of a fixed
28:rollout budget to multi-strength biased rollouts. Using con-
29:trolled task-specific language to isolate solver adaptation from
30:open-ended language robustness, we evaluate a CVRP policy
31:on road closures, time windows, vehicle assignments, visit-
32:order requirements, all pairwise combinations, and twelve
33:additional constraint types. LangNCO achieves 100% fea-
34:sibility on the four primary datasets with mean reference
35:gaps of 1.63–3.26%, 97.33–100% feasibility when separately
36:generated rules are composed for pairwise constraints, and
37:98.67% feasibility across the additional constraints. These re-
38:sults demonstrate modular post-training adaptation of a frozen
39:NCO solver through inspectable language-derived rules, with-

228:Method
229:Problem Setting
230:Let x be a capacitated vehicle routing problem (CVRP) in-
231:stance on nodes V = {0, 1, . . . , n}, where node 0 is the
232:depot, customer i has demand di, and vehicle capacity is Q.
233:A solution τ consists of depot-to-depot routes that visit ev-
234:ery customer exactly once without exceeding capacity. With
235:edge cost cij, its travel cost is C(τ | x).
236:A pretrained autoregressive NCO policy πθ selects the

921:Conclusion and Future Work
922:We introduced LangNCO, a framework for adapting a frozen
923:NCO solver to instance-specific natural-language constraints
924:at inference time. An LLM compiles each requirement into
925:a schema-validated DSL rule, which a deterministic Rule
926:Executor applies as an action mask. Critical-Offset Decod-
927:ing further uses rule-derived critical nodes to mitigate future
928:deadlocks within a fixed rollout budget. In the controlled de-
